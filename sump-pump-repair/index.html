Emergency

Pump not working while water rises

If the pit is filling and the pump is silent or humming without moving water, a basement flood can follow within hours. Electrical hazards add risk, so be careful.

  1. Do not step into water if outlets, appliances or the panel may be wet.
  2. Unplug the pump only if you can reach the outlet from dry ground.
  3. Move belongings, boxes and electronics off the floor.
  4. Use a wet vac or bucket to hold the level while you wait if it is safe.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and describe the sound the pump makes, if any.
Failure modes

Why a sump pump stops working

Most failures fall into a short list, and a plumber checks each one.

  • A stuck or tangled float switch.
  • A clogged intake screen or debris in the impeller.
  • A failed check valve that lets water drain back into the pit.
  • A burned-out motor after years of heavy use.
  • A tripped breaker, GFCI or power outage with no backup.
  • A frozen or blocked discharge line outside.

Local conditions

Why Pleasant Grove basements get wet

Pleasant Grove sits below the Wasatch Mountains, and spring snowmelt and heavy rain can raise groundwater around foundations. Some neighborhoods on the lower benches and near creek channels see seasonal seepage that others never do. Utah County also sits in an area where basement and crawl space moisture is a common concern.

A pump handles water that reaches the pit. If water enters through floor cracks or walls elsewhere, the fix may include drainage work, and we will tell you straight if you need basement waterproofing.

Maintenance

Keeping a pump ready before the storm

Test the pump twice a year by pouring a bucket of water into the pit and watching it start, empty and shut off. Clear debris from the pit and check that the discharge line runs well away from the foundation. If the pump is more than seven to ten years old, plan for replacement rather than wait for a failure.

Discharge

Where sump water may go

A sump discharge line should carry water well away from the foundation and not into the sanitary sewer or onto a neighbor's lot. Local storm water rules apply, so ask Pleasant Grove public works about your property before you reroute a line.
