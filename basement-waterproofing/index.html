Basement Waterproofing in Pleasant Grove, UT

A basement that seeps in spring or after storms needs a plan, not another coat of paint. Basement waterproofing in Pleasant Grove starts with finding how water gets in, then using drainage, a sump pump and exterior fixes to move it away from the foundation.

Source

Find the source before choosing a fix

Water enters through a few common paths: the joint where the floor meets the wall, cracks in the slab, window wells and walls that wick groundwater. Which one applies decides what works. Waterproofing without knowing the source is the most common way people waste money.

We start by looking at timing. Seepage after a rain points to surface water and grading. Seepage in spring melt points to rising groundwater. Seepage at all times may mean a leaking pipe, and leak detection is the right first step.

Checklist

Fixes that cost little to try first

Before a full system, check the simple things that move water away from the house.

  • Extend downspouts several feet from the foundation.
  • Regrade soil so it slopes away from the walls.
  • Clean window wells and check their drains.
  • Seal obvious slab cracks and wall penetrations.
  • Keep sprinklers from spraying against the foundation.

Local conditions

Groundwater and soils around Pleasant Grove

Pleasant Grove lies at the foot of the Wasatch Range, and snowmelt and heavy storms can raise the water table near foundations. Some lower areas see seasonal seepage that higher neighborhoods never do. Clay and mixed bench soils drain at different rates, which is one reason neighbors can have very different experiences.

Crawl spaces matter too. A damp crawl space can support mold and rot, and a drain, a pump or a vapor barrier may be part of the answer.

Honest limits

What plumbers do and what they do not

We install drains, sump pumps, discharge lines and backflow protection, and we diagnose plumbing leaks. Foundation cracks that move, bowed walls and large grading projects belong to a foundation or excavation contractor. If you need one, we will say so rather than sell a drain that cannot fix the cause.

Gradual seepage is generally not a covered loss, though sudden failures may be. A dry basement protects stored items and finished space, and it matters to buyers.

What to do

What to do while water is coming in

If water is entering your basement right now, these steps keep people safe and limit damage.

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, appliances or the panel could be wet, and turn off power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • Move boxes, electronics and furniture to higher ground.
  • Do not run gas appliances in a flooded space, and leave the house if you smell gas.
  • Use a wet vacuum or a pump to hold the level, and keep the discharge hose away from the foundation.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer before you clean up.
