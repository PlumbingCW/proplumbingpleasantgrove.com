Seasons

Winter, thaw and summer: when Highland calls us

Highland sits at about 4,876 feet, higher than most of Pleasant Grove, so freezes arrive earlier and last longer. Pipes in garages, exterior walls and crawl spaces split in cold snaps, and the damage often shows on the thaw. Early winter and the first warm days of spring bring burst pipe repair calls.

Summer brings a different load. Big yards and heavy irrigation draw hard on water systems, and a leaking line can run up a bill before anyone notices. Fall is the time to disconnect hoses and check outdoor spigots, a job our faucet repair page covers.

Before we arrive

Burst pipe or major leak in Highland: do this first

Larger homes have more rooms below a leak, so shutting off the water quickly matters even more.

  1. Shut off the main water valve, often in a basement, garage or utility closet.
  2. Open the lowest tap to release pressure.
  3. Switch off power to affected rooms at the breaker only if it is dry and safe to reach.
  4. Move valuables out of the water path and photograph the damage.
  5. Call (385) 488-1892 and say where the water is coming from.
Local note

Highland water and your yard

Highland incorporated in 1977 and has about 19,300 residents. Its pressurized irrigation system draws on American Fork Canyon water and the Murdock Canal, and the city has noted that canal water brings sediment that clogs sprinkler filters. Irrigation is separate from your indoor water, but a clogged yard filter can cause pressure problems in the yard system that people mistake for a house problem. Clean it first, then call if the house pressure is low too.

Source type: Highland City public notice on pressurized irrigation

Larger homes

Bigger homes, longer pipe runs

Many Highland homes are larger than average and have more bathrooms, longer supply runs and sometimes more than one water heater. Long runs mean slow hot water and more places for a fitting to fail. Recirculating pumps and a second water heater are common upgrades, and our water heater installation page covers sizing.

Code

Permits and code for larger Highland homes

Larger homes often have more fixtures, more water heaters and longer pipe runs, and all of it falls under the International Plumbing Code, which Utah applies statewide with amendments. Permit rules are set by Highland City, so ask before you add a bathroom or replace a water heater.
