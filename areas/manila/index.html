The area

Where Manila sits in Pleasant Grove

The Pleasant Grove General Plan names a "Manila Neighborhood" in the far northern part of the city. It describes older agricultural homes alongside newer estate lots and designates the area Rural Residential, with a minimum lot size of one half acre. The plan does not publish a street boundary, so treat the exact edges as approximate.

The city's own water notes give one useful anchor. Water supplied north of 1800 North is listed as Manila water. Real estate listings with Pleasant Grove addresses in this part of town show lots of roughly half an acre to an acre, on streets numbered in the 2800 North to 4200 North range.

This page is about the north end of the city. Past the northern edge, American Fork is the neighboring town, and the rest of Pleasant Grove is covered on the Pleasant Grove homepage.

Hard water

How hard the water is, by part of Pleasant Grove

Pleasant Grove publishes water hardness by source area in its public works FAQ. The USGS calls anything above 180 mg/L very hard, and one grain per gallon is about 17 mg/L.

What it means

What very hard water means for a water heater

The USGS notes that heating hard water leaves calcium carbonate scale, which can shorten equipment life and raise heating costs. Manila's figure is the highest the city lists, so tank sediment and scale on fixtures are fair things to ask about.

That does not mean every home here has a scale problem or a failing heater. It means a flush, an anode check and a conversation about water softening and filtration are reasonable questions to raise. If a tank is leaking or no longer heats, see water heater repair before the problem spreads to the floor.

Big lots

Plumbing questions that come with a larger property

These are general points, not claims about any one home in Manila.

  • On the street side of the meter, the city Water Department fixes leaks. On the house side, the owner is responsible. On a half acre or more, that owner side can be a long run, which is where leak detection and water line repair come in.
  • A wet patch in a lawn or a water bill that climbs with no change in use deserves a look. Do not guess that it is the sprinklers.
  • Pressurized irrigation is a separate system that carries untreated water for outdoor use only. It is not connected to indoor plumbing and it is not safe to drink.
  • Ask the city whether your property is on the city sewer. This page does not assume either way, and the Sewer Department number is (801) 785-2941.
Local note

Utilities worth knowing

The city says the owner is responsible for the lateral that serves the home, and that the Water Department handles leaks on the street side of the meter. It also says tampering with the meter or meter box is against the law, and the valve just inside the house is the one to use in an emergency.

The city runs its pressurized irrigation system from mid-April, with water available May 1, and shuts the main valves on October 4. Draining the home side before cold weather helps protect the lines.

Source type: Pleasant Grove public works FAQ and secondary water page
