The area

What the city plan says about downtown and midtown

The Pleasant Grove General Plan describes Downtown as the civic center, with City Hall, the police and fire station and the library. It calls Historic Main Street the heart of Downtown and says it is among the best preserved historic downtowns in the region.

Next to it, the plan names a Midtown Residential area. It says this area holds most of the city's historic housing stock and mixes single-family homes, duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes and planned developments. The plan does not fix a street boundary, so this page uses Main Street and the older grid of blocks around it as a general guide.

Local history sites list a group of seven similar houses built between 1902 and 1908 along the 100 and 200 South blocks. Their presence shows how old parts of this grid are. It does not tell us what is inside any wall, and we do not assume it.

Older homes

Questions that fit an older house

Age alone does not tell you what pipes a house has. These are checks, not predictions.

  • Have the sewer lateral from the house to the city main looked at with a camera before you buy, remodel or after a repeat backup. See sewer camera inspection.
  • If a drain slows down again soon after being cleared, ask what the camera found before agreeing to repeat cleanings. See drain cleaning.
  • Replacement of old supply lines is worth pricing when a house has a history of leaks. Repiping is a bigger job, so confirm the pipe material first.
  • If a line is damaged, sewer line repair options depend on depth, access and what the camera shows.
Property types

Who is responsible for which pipe

The city says maintenance and repair of the lateral that serves a home is the owner's responsibility. These are common setups in a mixed area like this one.

Backups

A backup in a lower fixture

The city public works FAQ says the city clears blockages in its main sewer lines and asks residents to call (801) 785-2941 for a mainline backup. If the main is not blocked, a plumbing or sewer contractor should check the lateral.

The same FAQ notes that the International Plumbing Code (Section 715, as cited by the city) requires a backwater valve on drains serving fixtures below the next upstream manhole. That is a question to raise before finishing a basement, especially in older buildings. A plumber can look at what your own fixtures and manhole levels call for.

Local note

Water here

The city lists water hardness at 17.5 grains per gallon for the area west of 500 East and south of 1800 North, which by the street numbers should include Main Street and most of the downtown grid. Confirm your own address with the city. That is very hard by USGS classes, so scale on fixtures and in water heaters is a fair thing to ask about.

Source type: Pleasant Grove public works FAQ and USGS hardness classes
