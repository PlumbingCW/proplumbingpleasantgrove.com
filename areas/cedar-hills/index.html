The neighborhood

A young city on a bench above Pleasant Grove

Cedar Hills was incorporated in 1977 with only 31 households, on land that had been a turkey ranch. Growth took off in the 1990s, and the city passed 9,000 residents by 2006 and is among the most densely populated in Utah County. Most homes here are only a few decades old, with PEX or copper supply lines and PVC drains.

Newer homes bring different calls than a century-old town. Water heaters and fixtures wear out in their second decade, and hard water leaves scale. Pipe failures from age are less common, but builder-grade fittings and supply lines do fail.

Before we arrive

Active leak or no hot water in Cedar Hills: first steps

A leak behind a finished wall can run unseen for a day. Act first, and let the plumber find the cause.

  1. Close the main water valve, or the valve at the water heater if only hot water is affected.
  2. If a tank is leaking, turn off the heater and set the gas control to pilot or off.
  3. Place towels or a bucket and keep people away from the wet area.
  4. Call (385) 488-1892 and say if you smell gas. If you do, leave the house and call 911.

Local note

Irrigation and indoor plumbing

Cedar Hills runs a pressurized irrigation system that is separate from the culinary water inside the house. Yard lines are not part of your indoor plumbing, and a broken sprinkler line is handled differently from a burst house pipe. The city lists an after-hours emergency line for water, sewer and roads at 801-420-2243, which is the right call for a main in the street. Hillside

Slopes, pressure and drainage

Cedar Hills homes sit at different elevations, and pressure can differ from lot to lot. High pressure stresses fittings and supply lines, and a failing regulator can burst a line. We test pressure on any call that involves repeated leaks.

Sloped lots also push surface water toward foundations. If you see damp at a basement wall in spring, check downspouts and grading first, then read about sump pump repair if a pit is already in place. For neighbors across the line, see Highland and American Fork.

Code

What the plumbing code means for Cedar Hills homes

The International Plumbing Code, applied across Utah with state amendments, requires a pressure reducing valve when static pressure goes above 80 psi. That matters on Cedar Hills lots at different elevations, where pressure can differ from street to street. Ask Cedar Hills city staff which permits apply to your project.
