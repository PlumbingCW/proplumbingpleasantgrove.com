Emergency

Signs the service line has failed

A broken line from the meter can drain thousands of gallons and flood a yard or crawl space. Quick action limits damage and water loss.

  1. Close the main shutoff valve in the house. If it will not turn or leaks, tell us.
  2. Look for wet or sunken ground, or water rising from the lawn.
  3. Check whether neighbors also lost water, which points to the city main instead.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and describe where the wet area sits relative to the meter.
Who owns what

Your line versus the city line

The city owns the main in the street and generally the meter. The line from the meter to the house belongs to you. If water is surfacing at the street or near the meter, call Pleasant Grove Public Works, and a plumber can confirm which side of the meter the leak is on.

Knowing the boundary saves money. A leak on the city side is not yours to pay for, and a plumber can tell you in a few minutes.

Pressure

Low pressure: what we check

Low pressure has several possible causes. A plumber narrows them down in order.

  • A partly closed main or meter valve.
  • A failing pressure regulator, common on homes with a pressure reducing valve.
  • Scale or corrosion narrowing old galvanized or steel pipe.
  • A leak in the service line that bleeds off flow.
  • A clogged fixture screen or cartridge, which affects one tap, not the whole house.

Pleasant Grove

Local conditions for buried lines

Frost depth, rocky bench soils and mature tree roots all affect buried lines in Pleasant Grove. A service line laid shallow can freeze in a hard winter, and roots can crush older pipe. In many older neighborhoods the original service line is galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside and slowly restricts flow.

If your home has low pressure that has worsened over the years, ask us to check the line material. Replacement can restore pressure and remove a source of rust-colored water.

Pressure

Pressure readings tell the real story

A gauge on a hose bib shows the pressure arriving at the house. Pressure above 80 psi stresses fittings, and the International Plumbing Code, applied in Utah with state amendments, calls for a pressure reducing valve above that level. Pressure that is low instead points to a restriction in the line.
