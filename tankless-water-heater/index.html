Is it right for you

When tankless makes sense and when it does not

Tankless units heat water as it flows, so they never run out, but each unit has a flow limit. A single unit can usually serve two showers at once, while three bathrooms running together may need a larger model or two. They shine in small utility rooms and for households that use steady amounts of hot water.

They are a weaker fit if you have a very long pipe run, a small gas line or a house where mineral scale is severe. We ask about all three before suggesting one.

Gas and venting

What a tankless install has to change

Most tankless installs need more than a swap. These are the upgrades that decide the cost.

  • A larger gas line, often 3/4 inch instead of 1/2 inch, to feed the high burner rate.
  • New venting, usually stainless or PVC for condensing units, with a condensate drain.
  • A dedicated electrical outlet for the fan and controls.
  • Isolation valves so the unit can be flushed in minutes without cutting pipes.
  • A recirculation plan if the fixture is far from the heater.
Flush and descale

Why tankless flushes matter in Utah County

Pleasant Grove's 2023 water quality report lists dissolved solids of 164 to 396 parts per million, and mineral water leaves scale inside a tankless heat exchanger. Scale narrows the passages, raises the temperature the unit must work against and eventually triggers error codes or cold bursts.

A yearly flush with a pump and descaling solution takes a few hours and clears the buildup. Our callers ask about this more than almost any other tankless topic, because manufacturers usually require it to keep warranties valid.

Planning

Tankless in apartments, rentals and small homes

Multi-unit buildings can use a bank of tankless heaters or a single central unit. Small homes in Pleasant Grove often benefit most because a wall-hung unit reclaims a closet. Homes that also have a sluggish main drain should sort that out first, so ask about drain cleaning while a plumber is on site.

Warranty and insurance

Many manufacturers require annual flushing for a full warranty. Insurance does not cover scale damage, because it is a maintenance issue, not a sudden event.

Code

Venting and installation rules

Tankless units are installed to the maker's instructions and to the plumbing code, which Utah applies through the International Plumbing Code with state amendments. Venting length, clearances and combustion air are the usual inspection points, so a plan should settle them before any unit is bought.
