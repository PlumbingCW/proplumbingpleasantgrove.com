Emergency

What to do when plumbing stops a business

A backup in a restaurant or a burst line in a shop can force a closure, and health codes may apply. Fast action protects the building and your customers.

  1. Shut off water at the main valve if water is flowing where it should not.
  2. Stop using sinks, restrooms and dishwashers if drains are backing up.
  3. Move stock and equipment out of the water path and cover what you cannot move.
  4. Photograph the damage for insurance, and note the time you first noticed it.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and say the type of business and the fixtures affected.

Pleasant Grove

Local context for businesses

Pleasant Grove has businesses along State Street, North County Boulevard and in its historic downtown, and plumbing ages vary from older storefronts to newer retail. Older buildings often have cast iron drains that crack, while newer builds use PVC. A restaurant or food business also faces grease rules, and many cities require a grease interceptor. Ask Pleasant Grove Public Works about current requirements.

The wastewater from the city is treated by the Timpanogos Special Service District, so a backup that affects several businesses on a block may be the city main.

Code

Commercial buildings follow the same plumbing code

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) statewide, with state amendments, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. Commercial work adds items such as grease interceptors for food businesses, backflow protection and fixture counts that depend on how the building is used. Ask Pleasant Grove's building department about your occupancy before you plan changes.
