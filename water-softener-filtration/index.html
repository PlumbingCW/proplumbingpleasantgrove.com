Test first

Why testing comes before buying

Water treatment is easy to oversell. A softener fixes hardness. It does not remove most contaminants, and a filter that targets chlorine will not soften water. Without a test, you can buy the wrong system.

Pleasant Grove publishes an annual water quality report, and a home test with a lab kit tells you about your tap in particular. We start there and then match the system to the result.

Signs

Signs your water needs treatment

Hardness and other issues leave clear marks. These are the usual ones.

  • White crust on faucets, shower heads and kettles.
  • Spotted glasses and dishes after the dishwasher.
  • Dry skin and stiff laundry.
  • Reduced flow from scaled aerators and pipes.
  • A water heater that gets noisy or loses capacity.
  • A taste or odor that stays after the tap runs.
Local water

What to know about water in Pleasant Grove

Utah County has hard water in many areas, and Pleasant Grove draws on a mix of sources. The Utah Geological Survey has published work on groundwater quality across the county, and the city's annual consumer confidence report lists tested values. We do not quote a hardness number for your home, since it varies by source and season. A test at your tap gives the real figure.

Scale also matters for your water heater. Hard water builds a layer on the tank bottom and the heating element, which cuts efficiency. See water heater repair for the symptoms.

Salt and drainage

What a softener needs from your home

A softener needs a drain for its regeneration water, an outlet and space near the main line. Some local rules and some neighborhoods limit salt discharge, so ask the city about current requirements before you buy. We handle the plumbing hookup and bypass valve so you can shut the system off when you need to.

Softeners need salt, and filters need new cartridges on schedule. Ask about the ongoing cost before you buy, and ask what happens if a part fails.

Standards

Certifications worth looking for

NSF/ANSI Standard 44 covers cation exchange water softeners, and other NSF/ANSI standards cover filters for chlorine taste, particles and specific contaminants. Look for the certification on the exact model you are considering. Public water systems also publish an annual consumer confidence report under the Safe Drinking Water Act.
