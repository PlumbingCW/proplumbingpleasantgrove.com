Safety

Clearing a jam safely

Turn off the switch and unplug the unit or cut power at the breaker before you touch anything. Look into the opening with a flashlight and use tongs or pliers to lift out any bone, fruit pit or utensil. Never put fingers in the opening.

Most disposals have a socket for a hex key at the bottom. Turning it back and forth frees the flywheel, and the red reset button on the unit may need a press afterward. If it jams again, the motor or bearing may be failing.

Care

What not to put in a disposal

Most disposal trouble starts with what goes down the drain, so these habits help.

  • Grease, oil and fat, which coat the pipe and harden.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in volume, which pack into sludge.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and clump.
  • Fibrous vegetables such as celery or corn husks.
  • Bones and large fruit pits, unless the unit is rated for them.
Replace or repair

When a new disposal is the better choice

A disposal lasts roughly eight to fifteen years. A leaking housing cannot be fixed, since the leak is the body failing. Repeated jams and a motor that overheats also signal the end.

If you are replacing one, we can size the horsepower to your household and check the drain trap and dishwasher connection while the sink is open. See plumbing installation for related fixture work.

Local water

Mineral scale and disposals

Utah County water leaves mineral scale inside disposal chambers and drain lines. Heavy scale can stall a motor and slow drains. Running cold water while the unit runs, and flushing with ice and citrus peel now and then, keeps the chamber cleaner.
