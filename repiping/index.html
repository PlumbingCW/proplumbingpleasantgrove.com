Whole House Repiping in Pleasant Grove, UT

Repiping replaces the supply pipes inside your walls, and in Pleasant Grove it is most common in older homes with galvanized steel or aging copper. If you have rusty water, falling pressure or leaks that keep coming back one fitting at a time, a repipe can be cheaper than years of repairs.

The decision

When repiping makes sense

A single failed fitting is a repair. A pattern of failures is a signal. If a plumber has patched three or four leaks in the last few years and the pipe wall looks pitted or crusted, the rest of the run is at similar risk.

Rusty or brown water at the tap, steadily falling pressure and pipes that bang or whistle are other clues. We look at the pipe, test pressure and explain what we see before suggesting anything.

Signs

Signs your pipes are near the end

Any two or three of these together make a repipe worth pricing.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in copper or leaks at galvanized joints.
  • Brown or orange water after a night of no use.
  • Low pressure at several fixtures that gets worse each year.
  • Visible green or white crust on exposed pipes.
  • A home built before the 1960s that still has original steel pipes.
Galvanized steel

Galvanized pipe in older Pleasant Grove homes

Many homes built in the early and middle twentieth century used galvanized steel supply lines. The zinc coating wears off over time, and rust builds inside until the opening narrows. That is why a house with galvanized pipe can have plenty of pressure at the street and a weak trickle in the shower.

Replacing galvanized pipe fixes the pressure problem and removes a source of rust in the water. It also removes the mix of dissimilar metals that corrodes joints in partly updated systems.

Value and insurance

A repipe can reduce repeat leaks and insurance claims, though policies vary. Some insurers ask about galvanized or polybutylene pipe when writing coverage, so ask yours.

Code

Approved materials and inspections

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) statewide, with state amendments, under Utah Code Title 15A, Chapter 3, Part 3. It lists the pipe materials approved for drinking water, and a repipe is normally permitted and inspected. Ask the Pleasant Grove building department what your project needs, and keep the inspection sign off for your records.
