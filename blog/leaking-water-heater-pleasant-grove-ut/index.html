Leaking Hot Water Heater: What to Do in Pleasant Grove, UT

If your water heater is leaking, turn off the power or gas, close the cold water valve above it and call a plumber. A leak from a fitting or a relief valve can be repaired, but a leak from the tank itself means the tank has failed, and the heater needs replacing. Do not wait, since a small leak can become a flood.

Here are the steps and how to tell which leak you have.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
Emergency

First steps for a leaking water heater

A leaking heater can scald, flood or create an electrical hazard. These steps come first.

  1. Turn off the power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater's control to off or pilot.
  2. Close the cold water supply valve on top of the heater.
  3. Do not touch a leaking electric heater if you are standing in water.
  4. Place a bucket or pan, and open a hot tap to relieve pressure.
  5. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911, then call us.

The place the water appears tells you whether a repair is possible.

Tank body or bottomCorrosion through the tankReplace the heater
Condensation on the tankCold water and humid airNot a leak, wipe and monitor

Water pooling under the tank with no fitting leak almost always means the tank has failed.

Why tanks fail

What kills a tank heater

Inside a steel tank, a rod called an anode corrodes first to protect the tank walls. Once the rod is used up, the tank itself begins to rust. Sediment from hard water settles in the bottom, insulates the water from the burner and speeds the damage.

Most tank heaters last about ten to fifteen years. If yours is older and leaking from the tank, repair is not possible. See water heater repair for the cases that can be fixed.

Plan ahead

Signs a tank is near the end

Replacing a heater before it leaks costs less than cleaning up after it does.

  • Rust colored hot water.
  • Rumbling or popping noises from sediment.
  • Moisture or rust at the base of the tank.
  • A heater over ten years old, especially in hard water.
  • Fluctuating hot water temperature.
Local note

Local water and scale

Utah County water carries dissolved minerals, and heaters in Pleasant Grove homes often show sediment in the bottom of the tank. Flushing a tank once a year removes some of it. Pleasant Grove requires permits for most water heater replacement, so ask the building department what applies to your job, and note that gas appliances need proper venting and a safe gas connection.

Costs and cleanup

What happens after the leak

If a tank has leaked, check the floor and the wall behind it for dampness. Dry wet materials within a day or two to prevent mold. Photograph the damage for your insurer. Many policies cover sudden leaks, but not damage from slow leaks.

A plumber can replace a tank heater in a few hours in most cases. For a tankless alternative, tankless water heaters are an option if you can plan. If your home is near Cedar Hills or Highland, see Cedar Hills and Highland for where we dispatch.

Choosing a replacement

What to decide when the heater has failed

A failed heater forces a quick decision, and a few questions make it easier.

  • How many people use hot water, and at what times.
  • Whether you want to stay with gas or electric, or change fuel.
  • Whether a tankless unit is worth the extra cost and venting work.
  • Where the heater sits, and whether it needs a drain pan.
  • What warranty the heater carries on the tank and the parts.
Prevent a repeat

Keeping the next heater from leaking

Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment, and check the anode rod every few years, since a spent rod lets the tank rust. Test the relief valve by lifting the lever briefly, and call a plumber if it drips or will not reseat.

Install a drain pan with a drain line to a floor drain, so a small leak runs to a safe place. For a heater in a finished area, a water alarm on the floor gives early warning of a leak.

Electric or gas

Safety differences between electric and gas heaters

An electric heater leaks onto live heating elements, so the first step is to cut power at the breaker. Do not touch a leaking electric heater while standing in water. A gas heater needs its gas control turned to off or pilot, and any smell of gas means you should leave and call for help.

Both types need the cold water supply closed to stop the flow. After that, a plumber confirms the source of the leak, checks the venting on a gas unit and tells you whether repair or replacement makes sense. Replacement is the usual answer when the tank itself is leaking.

Call us

When to call the 24/7 line

Call right away if water is pooling around the heater, if the leak is from the tank or if you smell gas or see scorched wiring. Call during business hours for a slow drip at a fitting or a damp relief valve pipe.

Either way, tell the dispatcher the age of the heater, the fuel type and where the water is coming from. That lets the plumber bring the right parts, and it speeds the repair or replacement.

Code

Why the relief valve exists

The temperature and pressure relief valve is a required safety device under the International Plumbing Code, which Utah applies statewide with amendments. If it discharges, the tank is too hot or the pressure is too high. Do not cap the discharge pipe, and have a plumber find the cause.
