Tankless Heat Exchanger Problems in Pleasant Grove, UT

The heat exchanger is the part of a tankless water heater that actually warms the water, and it is the part most hurt by hard water. Scale narrows its passages, the unit overheats or throws error codes, and in time the exchanger can leak. Descaling fixes early problems, but a leaking or cracked exchanger usually means a repair or a new unit.

Here is how to tell which you have.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
The part

What the heat exchanger does

A tankless unit has a burner and a heat exchanger, a set of narrow copper or stainless passages that carry water past the flame. Water enters cold and leaves hot in seconds. Because the passages are narrow, anything that builds up inside restricts flow and heat transfer.

Many units have two exchangers, a primary and a secondary, and a failure in one may leave the other working. A plumber with the model number can tell which part you have.

Symptoms

Signs of a scaled or failing exchanger

These symptoms often appear in stages. Early ones respond to descaling.

  • Hot water that runs lukewarm or fluctuates between hot and cold.
  • Error codes about overheating, flow or ignition.
  • A kettle-like rumbling or popping from the unit.
  • Reduced flow at the tap even though supply pressure is fine.
  • Water dripping from the bottom of the unit, which can mean a cracked exchanger or a loose fitting.
Hard water

How hard water kills heat exchangers

Dissolved calcium and magnesium come out of water when it is heated and form scale on the hottest surfaces. In a tankless unit, that surface is the exchanger. A thin layer of scale insulates the metal and forces the burner to run hotter and longer.

Utah County water carries dissolved minerals, and Pleasant Grove homes often see scale on fixtures. A home test tells you how hard your tap water is. See water softener and filtration for how to test before buying a system.

Descaling

How descaling works

Descaling is a routine job that a plumber or a careful homeowner can do with the right kit.

1

Isolate the unit

Close the isolation valves so water and gas are cut off from the lines.

2

Connect a pump

Attach hoses to the service ports and a small pump and bucket.

3

Circulate cleaner

Run a food-safe descaling solution through the exchanger for the time the maker advises.

4

Flush and test

Rinse with clean water, reopen valves and check for leaks and normal temperature.

Repair or replace

When to repair and when to replace

The choice depends on the damage and the age of the unit.

ConditionUsual response
Light scale, error codes clear after descalingDescale and set up yearly service
Heavy scale, repeated overheatingDescale, then check the exchanger for damage
Cracked or leaking exchangerReplace the exchanger if the unit is young, or the whole unit
Unit past fifteen years with multiple faultsReplace, see water heater replacement

Warranty terms often require proof of yearly descaling, so keep your service records.

Prevention

Keeping a tankless unit healthy

Flush the unit once a year, and more often if your water is hard. Clean the inlet filter, check the vent and keep the area around the unit clear. A softener reduces scale but must be maintained too.

If you are weighing a swap, water heater repair explains the basic checks. For neighbors in nearby cities, our pages for Highland and Cedar Hills show where we dispatch.

Error codes

Reading the signals before you call

Most tankless units show a code on the panel when they sense trouble. Write the code down, along with the make and model, before you call. Many codes point to flow, ignition or temperature problems, and a plumber can often tell from the code whether scale is the cause.

Do not reset the unit again and again. Repeated restarts can hide a developing fault and stress the burner. If the code returns after one reset, leave it off and call.

Service record

What to keep for warranty and resale

A tidy record helps with warranty claims and shows a buyer the unit was cared for.

  • The date of each descaling and who did it.
  • The model and serial number from the label.
  • Photos of the unit and the vent before and after service.
  • Receipts for filters, cleaners and parts.
  • Any error codes that appeared and what fixed them.
Cost of waiting

Why early service costs less than late repair

A scaled heat exchanger works harder every month. The burner runs longer to reach the same temperature, gas use goes up and the metal sees more heat stress. A routine descale costs little, and a new exchanger or a replacement unit costs far more.

Ask for service when you first notice slow heating or an error code, not after a failure. If the unit is older and the maker no longer stocks parts, compare repair quotes with the price of a new unit, and ask about the remaining warranty.

Bottom line

What to do this month

Check your manual for the descaling schedule, write down the model and serial number and book a service visit if it has been over a year. Clean the inlet filter while you are at it, and keep the area around the unit clear.

If you see error codes or lukewarm water, do not wait. Early service is cheap, and a neglected exchanger is not.
