Tankless Water Heaters for Apartments in Pleasant Grove, UT

Tankless water heaters can work in an apartment building, but the right layout depends on the size of the building. A small building often does well with one tankless unit per apartment, while a larger one may need a central system with a recirculation loop. The deciding factors are gas capacity, venting and how many units run at once.

Here is how owners compare the choices.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
Two layouts

Central system or one unit per apartment

A central system serves the whole building from one mechanical room. It has fewer vents and one maintenance point, but it needs a recirculation loop so tenants far from the plant do not wait for hot water. Pipes and pumps add cost and heat loss.

A per-unit layout puts a small tankless heater in each apartment. Each tenant controls their own hot water, and a failure affects one unit. The trade-off is more vents, more gas meters or branches and more heaters to maintain.

Comparison

How the two layouts compare

Neither layout is always better. This table lists the factors owners weigh most.

Gas and venting

Gas capacity and venting come first

A tankless unit uses a lot of gas for a short time. In a building, the gas meter and the supply line must support every unit running at once, and many older buildings have a line sized for tank heaters. A gas utility and a licensed plumber must confirm capacity before you buy.

Venting is the other early check. Tankless units need either power vents or concentric direct vents, and a vent route through the roof or exterior wall. In a multi-story building, vent length and clearance can decide the layout before anything else.

Before you buy

Questions to answer before choosing a layout

Answering these first saves rework and keeps the quote realistic.

  • How many units and bathrooms does the building have, and how many peak at once.
  • What is the gas meter size, and will the gas utility upgrade it if needed.
  • Where can vents terminate, and what are the clearances to windows and property lines.
  • Do tenants pay for their own gas, or does the owner pay for hot water.
  • Who will service the system, and how fast can parts be had.
Winter

Cold incoming water in Utah winters

A tankless heater raises the temperature of incoming water by a fixed amount at a given flow. In winter, incoming water is colder, so the same unit delivers less flow at the same temperature. Pleasant Grove winters are cold enough that sizing should use a cold-weather design temperature, not a summer number.

If a building has a shared supply line, winter demand peaks in the morning when many tenants shower. That peak sizes the plant, so ask your plumber to size for the busiest winter morning.

Local note

What to check locally

Pleasant Grove adopts and enforces building and plumbing codes, and permits are required for gas and water heater work in most cases. Ask the city building department what applies to your building and whether the project needs plans reviewed. Utah requires licensed contractors for plumbing and gas work, and the state licensing division lets you check a license.

Source type: City of Pleasant Grove building department and Utah Division of Professional Licensing

Service and scale

Maintenance and water quality

Tankless heaters need yearly descaling in areas with hard water, and Utah County water carries dissolved minerals. A building owner should budget for flushing and for a filter or softener where scale is a problem. Our water softener and filtration page explains how to test first.

If you own several buildings, plan service across them. For a small building in a nearby town, our pages for Lindon and American Fork list where we dispatch, and water heater installation covers the standard process.

Tenant impact

Planning the swap around tenants

Changing a building's water heating affects every tenant, so plan the schedule early. A per-unit install needs access to each apartment, and a central plant needs a day or more with the building's hot water off. Give tenants written notice and a clear window, and ask the plumber for a realistic duration for each unit.

Keep a spare plan for a failed install day. A temporary heater or a staged schedule keeps tenants from going without hot water overnight.

Budget items

Costs owners forget to include

The heater price is only part of the bill. These items often decide whether a plan is affordable.

  • Gas meter or line upgrades from the utility.
  • Vent runs through roofs and exterior walls, including flashing and patching.
  • Electrical outlets and condensate drains for each unit.
  • Permits, plan review and inspection fees.
  • Descaling kits, filters and a yearly service plan for every heater.
Rules and records

Code, safety and paperwork for a building project

A building water heater is more than a purchase. Gas appliances need combustion air, correct venting and a gas shutoff within reach, and many cities require carbon monoxide alarms near fuel burning equipment. Ask the Pleasant Grove building department which standards apply to your building and which inspections are needed before tenants return to normal use.

Keep every permit, inspection sign off and manual in one folder for the building. A new owner, an insurer or a lender may ask for them, and a clear record also speeds any later repair.

Finally, post a simple notice by each heater with the shutoff locations and the number to call. In a building with many tenants, a clear label saves minutes when something goes wrong.
