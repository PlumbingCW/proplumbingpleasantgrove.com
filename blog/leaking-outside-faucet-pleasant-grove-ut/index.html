Leaking Outside Faucet Repair in Pleasant Grove, UT

An outside faucet leaks from one of three places: the spout, the handle or inside the wall. A spout drip usually needs a new washer, a handle leak usually needs a packing nut turned or replaced, and a leak inside the wall means a split pipe that needs a plumber. Shut the water to the spigot first, then find which one you have.

This guide shows how to tell them apart.

Diagnose

Where the water shows up and what it means

The location of the leak points to the cause.

Drips from the spout when offWorn washer or seatReplace the washer or the valve
Leaks around the handle stemLoose or worn packingTighten or replace the packing

Quick fixes

Fixing a drip or a handle leak

For a handle leak, first try tightening the packing nut just behind the handle with a wrench, a quarter turn at a time. If that does not stop it, the packing is worn and a replacement kit costs a few dollars. Turn the water off first and follow the maker's instructions.

For a spout drip, the washer inside the valve is worn. A plumber or a careful homeowner can replace it. If the valve body is corroded or the faucet is old, replacing the whole faucet is often better value.

Frost-proof spigots

Why frost-proof faucets still freeze

A frost-proof spigot has a long stem that puts the valve seat inside the warm part of the house. It only works if water drains out of the pipe when you shut it off. A hose left attached through winter traps water in the barrel, and that water freezes and splits the pipe.

The leak often shows only in spring, when the tap is first turned on. Water sprays inside the wall, and it can damage framing and insulation. If you see water at the base of the spigot or a wet stain on the inside wall, shut off the water to that line and call.

Emergency

If water is spraying inside the wall

A split pipe behind a wall can release a lot of water fast. Treat it as an emergency until the line is shut off.

  1. Close the main water valve or the dedicated shutoff for the outdoor line.
  2. Open a lower tap to drain pressure.
  3. Check the wall inside the house, in the garage or basement, for wetness.
  4. Call (385) 488-1892 and say that water is leaking inside a wall.
Winter prep

Steps to protect your spigots before freezing weather

A few minutes in the fall prevent the most common spring leak.

  • Disconnect every garden hose and splitter before the first hard freeze.
  • Close the interior shutoff to the outdoor line if you have one, and open the outdoor tap to drain the line.
  • Install an insulated cover over the spigot.
  • Check for drips in spring before you leave the water on.
Local note

Local timing

Utah winters bring hard freezes along the Wasatch Front, and Pleasant Grove homes at higher elevation on the bench freeze earlier than those in the valley. Utah County irrigation systems are usually shut down and drained in autumn, and city pressurized irrigation is separate from your house plumbing. Treat your spigot the same way and drain it before winter.

Source type: General Utah climate and plumbing practice

When to call

When a plumber is worth the call

If the spigot sprays inside the wall, if the stem is corroded or if you cannot find the shutoff, call a plumber. A simple replacement is quick, but a split pipe means opening the wall and patching it. Our burst pipe repair team handles this, and a hidden leak can be located with leak detection.

Spring check

A spring test before you use the hose

A two minute test in spring shows a winter split before it floods a wall.

  • Open the interior shutoff to the outdoor line if you have one.
  • Open the outdoor tap slowly and watch the base for water.
  • Check the inside of the wall, garage or basement near the spigot for wet spots.
  • Close the tap and watch the meter for a few minutes to see if it moves.
  • If you find a leak, shut the water off and call a plumber.
Replace or repair

When a new spigot is the better value

A spigot older than fifteen years, with a corroded body or a stem that no longer seals, is worth replacing. A new frost-proof model costs more than a washer kit, but it ends repeated repairs and fits the wall properly. Choose a length that matches your wall thickness so the valve sits inside the warm part of the house.

If you want a vacuum breaker, which prevents hose water from flowing back into your supply, many models include one. Ask your plumber which style fits your property and local requirements.

Water bill

What a leaking spigot does to your bill

A steady leak at a spigot adds up over a month, and in summer many households run the hose often enough to hide the extra use. Compare your water bill with the same month last year, and watch the meter with all indoor fixtures off.

A small repair now prevents a larger bill and a wet wall later. If you cannot find the source quickly, a leak detection visit finds it without opening walls.
