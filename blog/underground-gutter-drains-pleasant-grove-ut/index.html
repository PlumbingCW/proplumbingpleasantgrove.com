Cleaning Underground Gutter Drains in Pleasant Grove, UT

An underground gutter drain carries roof water from your downspouts through buried pipe to a safe outlet. When it clogs, water backs up at the gutter, overflows at the downspout and can soak the soil beside your foundation. You can often clear a simple clog with a hose and a plumber's snake, but a crushed or root-filled pipe needs a camera and a repair.

Here is how to handle each case.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
The system

How an underground gutter drain works

A buried gutter drain is a plastic pipe, often corrugated black or solid white, that connects each downspout to a common line. The line runs downhill to a pop-up emitter in the yard, a dry well, a curb outlet or a storm drain. Gravity moves the water, so a slope of at least a quarter inch per foot matters.

Because the pipe is buried, nothing warns you until water backs up. That is why one overflowing gutter during a storm is often the first sign.

Causes

Why underground drains clog

Most clogs trace to a short list of causes, and the debris tells you which one it is.

  • Leaves, needles and granules from roof shingles that wash into the downspout.
  • Roots that enter at a joint or a crack, common near mature trees.
  • Sediment that settles in a flat or bellied section.
  • A crushed pipe from a vehicle or heavy equipment over a shallow line.
  • An outlet blocked by soil, ice or a pop-up emitter that is stuck shut.
Clearing

How to flush a simple clog

Start with the lowest-risk method and work up.

1

Disconnect

Remove the downspout elbow so you can reach the pipe opening.

2

Hose it out

Push a garden hose into the pipe and run water to flush debris toward the outlet.

3

Snake it

Feed a drain snake or a plumber's auger and cut through the blockage.

4

Test

Run water and check that it reaches the outlet and flows freely.

Why it matters here

Foundation water in Pleasant Grove

Pleasant Grove sits at the foot of the Wasatch Range, and heavy storms and spring snowmelt can send a lot of water toward homes on the bench. A blocked roof drain dumps that water beside the foundation, and a basement can take it in. Many basement seepage problems start with gutters and grading, not groundwater.

If water already enters the basement, read about basement waterproofing to see how drains and pumps fit together.

Local note

Local rule worth knowing

Roof water should not run onto a neighbor's lot or into the sanitary sewer. Utah cities, including Pleasant Grove, set rules for storm water, and a downspout connected to the sewer line can overload it. Ask Pleasant Grove public works about local rules before you reroute a drain.

Source type: City of Pleasant Grove public works and stormwater guidance

Prevention

Keeping gutter drains open

Clean gutters in late spring and after leaf fall, and install screens or guards if you have mature trees. Check the outlet each season and keep it clear of soil and weeds. Pour water into each downspout once a year to confirm it flows.

Tools

What you need to flush a drain yourself

Most homeowner flushes need only basic tools, and a few minutes of setup.

  • A garden hose with a nozzle that fits the pipe opening.
  • A drain snake or a plumber's auger long enough for the run.
  • Work gloves, because debris is wet and sharp.
  • A bucket or tarp to catch leaves and silt.
  • A flashlight to look into the pipe and the outlet.
Camera look

Why a camera pays off for stubborn clogs

If a drain clogs again within a season, guesswork gets expensive. A small camera on a flexible rod shows whether the cause is a crushed section, a root, a joint that has separated or just silt in a flat run. Each calls for a different fix, and you avoid digging the wrong spot.

Ask the plumber for the footage and the distance from the downspout to the defect. With that, a repair can open a small patch of lawn instead of a long trench.

Seasonal routine

A simple yearly routine for gutters and drains

Plan three short checks each year. In late spring, clean gutters after the cottonwood and tree debris falls, and flush each downspout with a hose. In early fall, clear gutters again before leaf drop ends, and check the outlet or emitter for soil and weeds.

Before the first hard freeze, make sure the outlet drains so it does not hold standing water that freezes and blocks the pipe. Each check takes less than an hour, and together they keep roof water away from your foundation.

Bottom line

The one thing to check this week

Pour a bucket of water into your downspouts and watch where it goes. If it backs up, drains slowly or never appears at the outlet, you have found a problem before a storm does. Flush it with a hose, and call a plumber if it clogs again.

A few minutes now keeps roof water from soaking your foundation later.
