How to Install Sink Plumbing in a Pleasant Grove, UT Home

Installing sink plumbing means connecting two supply lines, a faucet, a trap and a drain to the wall, with the right slope and a vent so the drain flows quietly. Swapping a sink in the same spot is a reasonable weekend job. Adding a new sink or moving drains is a job for a licensed plumber and may need a permit.

This guide explains the parts and the steps.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
The parts

What makes up a sink plumbing setup

Every sink has the same basic parts. Two shutoff valves, called angle stops, sit on the wall or floor and control hot and cold water. Flexible supply lines run from the stops to the faucet. A drain assembly collects water at the sink, and a trap holds a seal of water that blocks sewer gas.

The trap connects to a drain pipe in the wall, and the drain must be vented so air can enter behind the water. Without a vent, the drain gurgles and can siphon the trap dry.

Process

Steps for a same-location sink swap

These steps assume the old sink is coming out and the new one goes in the same place.

1

Shut off water

Close both angle stops and open the faucet to drain the lines.

2

Disconnect

Remove supply lines, the trap and the old sink, and cap the drain with a rag.

3

Mount

Set the new sink and connect the faucet before you drop it in if you can.

4

Connect drain

Fit the drain assembly and trap, keeping the pipe sloping slightly downhill.

5

Test

Turn the water on slowly, check every joint and run water for several minutes.

Slope and vent

The two details that decide success

A horizontal drain pipe needs a gentle downhill slope, about a quarter inch per foot. Too flat and solids settle, too steep and water outruns solids. A trap too far from the vent causes siphoning and slow drains, and plumbing codes set a maximum distance.

If you are only replacing a sink in the same spot, the old rough in already handles these. If you move the sink or add one, the new run must be laid out right, and that is where a plumber saves trouble.

Local note

Permits and licenses in Utah

Utah requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed contractor in most cases, with limited exceptions for homeowners on their own residence. Permit rules vary by city, so ask the Pleasant Grove building department what applies to a sink install and whether an inspection is needed. A permit protects your resale value and your insurance.

Source type: Utah Division of Professional Licensing and City of Pleasant Grove building department

Hard water

Local water and fixture life

Utah County water leaves mineral scale on faucets, aerators and cartridges, and scale can clog a new faucet within months. Check the aerator when flow drops, and soak it in vinegar to clear it. If scale keeps returning, a softener or filter may be worth a look.

If a faucet leaks or drips after install, read our page on faucet repair. For a neighboring town job, see Lindon or Cedar Hills to see how we dispatch.

Tools and parts

What to gather before you start

A sink swap goes quickly when everything is on the counter before the water goes off.

  • Adjustable wrench, basin wrench and a bucket for the trap.
  • New braided supply lines sized to the faucet connections.
  • Plumber's putty or silicone, if the maker calls for it.
  • Thread seal tape for threaded joints that need it.
  • A flashlight, rags and a small container for old parts.
Leak test

How to test the finished job

Open each angle stop slowly and look at every joint with a dry paper towel pressed against it. A towel shows even a slow weep that the eye misses. Run the faucet for a few minutes, fill the basin and release it, and check the trap and drain joints again while it empties.

Check again after an hour and again the next morning. A joint that is dry at first can seep once the gasket settles, and a quick retighten usually fixes it before it damages the cabinet.

Know your limits

When to stop and call a plumber

A same-spot swap is a fair job for a careful homeowner. Stop and call a plumber if the shutoff valves will not close, if the old pipe is galvanized and breaks when you touch it or if the drain does not line up with the new trap. Forcing a bad connection tends to cause leaks inside the cabinet that you will not see for weeks.

Call a plumber too when the job involves a new location, a new drain line or a new vent. These need correct slope and venting to meet code, and a permit and inspection are often required. A short consultation before you begin costs far less than repairing a failed install.

A plumber can also leave you with good shutoff valves and fresh supply lines, which make every later repair easier.

Final tip

Photograph before you disconnect

Take a photo of the old connections before you remove anything. It shows how the supply lines and the trap were arranged, and it helps you match parts at the store. A phone picture also helps a plumber diagnose a problem if you need to call halfway through.

Label the hot and cold lines with tape, and keep old parts until the new sink has run dry for a day.

Code

Trap and vent rules from the model code

The International Plumbing Code, applied in Utah with state amendments, calls for a trap at each fixture and limits the distance between a trap and its vent. Those rules are why a P trap is standard and why a sink moved across a room needs a new vent plan.
