Signs You Need Sewer Line Repair in Pleasant Grove, UT

The main signs of a failing sewer line are drains that slow down in several fixtures at once, gurgling when you flush, a sewer smell indoors or outdoors and soggy or sunken patches in the yard. Any of these can mean a cracked, rooted or collapsed pipe. A camera inspection confirms the damage, and acting early is cheaper than cleaning up a backup.

This guide covers each sign and the next step.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
Indoor signs

Signs inside the house

Indoor signs usually appear first, and they often start small.

  • More than one fixture drains slowly at the same time.
  • Toilets gurgle or bubble when a tub or washer drains.
  • Water backs up in the lowest drain, often a basement floor drain.
  • A sewer smell near drains that does not clear.
  • Clogs that return within weeks of cleaning.
Outdoor signs

Signs in the yard and at the foundation

Outdoor signs point to a break in the buried line.

  • A lawn patch that is greener, wetter or lusher than the grass around it.
  • A sunken area or sinkhole along the line path.
  • Cracks in the foundation or slab, though these have other causes too.
  • A sewer odor near the cleanout or the yard.
  • Rodents or insects entering from the yard, which can follow a broken line.
Why lines fail

What damages sewer lines in Pleasant Grove

Roots are the leading cause in a city known for mature trees. They enter at joints and cracks and grow until they block the pipe. Age is another cause, since older clay and cast iron pipe crack, corrode and shift.

Soil movement and freeze and thaw cycles stress pipe, and a poorly bedded line may belly and collect waste. Heavy loads such as a driveway built over a line can crush it. Each cause leaves a different mark on a camera inspection.

Confirming

How a camera finds the problem

A plumber clears enough of the line to run a waterproof camera from a cleanout. The screen shows roots, cracks, offsets and low spots, and a counter marks the distance of each defect. You get video and a summary, and you see what the plumber sees.

A camera look is the best single step before a repair. Our sewer inspection page explains the process, and the camera often decides between cleaning and repair.

Repair options

What a repair can look like

The right choice depends on the finding.

FindingUsual response
Roots in a joint, pipe otherwise soundCut roots and monitor, or line the joint
Cracked sectionSpot repair or liner
Collapsed or offset sectionDig and replace the section
Whole line brittle or belliedReplace the line or line the full length

Utah's Blue Stakes 811 service must mark buried utilities before any excavation.

Local note

Who owns what in Pleasant Grove

The city owns the sewer main in the street, and wastewater from the city is treated by the Timpanogos Special Service District. You generally own the lateral from the house to the main. If a backup affects the whole street, call Pleasant Grove public works. If it affects only your house, the problem is likely on your lateral.

Source type: City of Pleasant Grove public works and Timpanogos Special Service District

Next steps

What to do if you see the signs

Do not wait for a full backup. Schedule a camera inspection, and if you are close to a backup, stop using water and call the 24/7 line. Early action keeps the repair small.

If you live across a border, see our pages for American Fork and Lindon to see where we dispatch. For a line that is blocked right now, sewer line cleaning opens it first.

Buying a home

Sewer checks when you buy a Pleasant Grove house

The sewer lateral is one of the most expensive hidden parts of a house, and a home inspection rarely looks at it.

  • Ask for a camera inspection during the inspection period.
  • Ask the seller about past backups, root cutting and repairs.
  • Find the cleanout and see whether it is accessible.
  • Look at the age of the home and the trees near the line.
  • Keep the video, since it supports a repair request or a price change.
Cost and timing

How repairs are priced and timed

Sewer repairs vary widely, because depth, length, access and surface restoration drive the price. A short spot repair in an open yard costs far less than a long replacement under a driveway. A plumber should give you a written price after the camera shows the real condition, and explain the alternatives.

Timing matters too. A repair planned in dry weather costs less and disrupts less than one done during an emergency backup. If the camera shows a defect but the line still flows, you can often schedule the work for a convenient week.

Acting early

Why an early repair beats an emergency backup

A damaged sewer line rarely fails all at once. Roots widen, joints shift and flow slows, and each week the odds of a backup rise. Repairing at the first sign lets you pick the date, compare methods and avoid cleanup in the house.

A backup brings extra costs that a planned repair does not. Cleanup, drying, damaged flooring and lost use of the house add up quickly, and many insurance policies limit coverage for sewer backups. A camera look now is a small cost compared with these risks.

Bottom line

What to remember

Slow drains in several fixtures, gurgling and outdoor wet spots are the signs to act on. A camera inspection confirms the problem, and an early repair costs less than a backup.

Know where your cleanout is, keep the number for the plumber handy and call the city if neighbors are affected too. These small steps shorten any emergency.

Code

Cleanouts and slope are code items

The model plumbing code, applied in Utah with state amendments, sets slope and cleanout requirements for drain lines. A lateral with no cleanout or a section that has lost its slope is a defect a camera can document, and it supports a request for repair or a price change.
