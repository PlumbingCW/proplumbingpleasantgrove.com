Baking Soda and Vinegar for Clogged Drains: Pleasant Grove, UT

Baking soda and vinegar can freshen a drain and loosen a light film of grease, but they rarely clear a real clog. The fizz is mostly water and sodium acetate, and it does not cut hair, roots or a wad of paper. Use it as a gentle maintenance step, and call a plumber when a drain stays slow or backs up.

Here is how to use it safely and what to expect.

By the Pleasant Grove Pro Plumbing teamPublished November 14, 2026Updated November 14, 2026
The chemistry

What happens when you mix them

Baking soda is a mild base and vinegar is a weak acid. Together they react and release carbon dioxide, which is the fizz you see. The mixture ends up as mostly water and a dissolved salt, so the fizz is short lived and does little work in a pipe.

A light film on the pipe wall may loosen, and the smell often improves. A clog made of hair, paper or a solid object stays where it is.

Safe method

How to use it on a slow drain

This method is safe for most pipe types. Do not combine it with a commercial drain cleaner.

1

Clear the sink

Remove standing water and any visible debris from the drain.

2

Add soda

Pour half a cup of baking soda into the drain.

3

Add vinegar

Pour half a cup of white vinegar and let it fizz for fifteen minutes.

4

Flush

Run hot tap water for a minute to rinse the pipe.

Better tools

What works better for clogs

These options are more effective, and most cost little.

  • A plastic hair snare that pulls hair from a tub or sink drain.
  • A cup or flange plunger with a good seal.
  • A small hand auger that reaches the trap and the first few feet of pipe.
  • Hot water flushes with a little dish soap for grease.
  • A plumber's cable or jetter when the clog is deeper.
Cautions

When not to use it

Do not pour vinegar and baking soda into a drain that already has a chemical drain cleaner in it. Mixing products can release fumes or cause splashing. Do not use it on a toilet that is rising, since it will not help and the fizz can push water up.

If you plan to have a plumber visit, tell them what you have used. Caustic cleaners are a safety risk for the person working on the drain, and our drain cleaning team will ask before they start.

Local note

Why local drains clog

Pleasant Grove homes drain into city mains, and a private lateral carries waste to the main. A clog that affects one fixture is local. A clog that affects several points to the lateral. Utah County water leaves mineral scale in pipes and traps, which can narrow drains over time, so occasional flushing helps.

Source type: City of Pleasant Grove public works and general plumbing practice

Prevention

Keeping drains clear

Strain food from the sink and keep grease out. Use a hair catcher in the tub and shower. Run hot water after you use the sink for greasy dishes.

If a drain keeps clogging, stop treating the symptom. A camera shows the cause, and a jetter can scour the pipe. See hydro jetting for repeat clogs, and our pages for Cedar Hills and American Fork if you serve a property there.

Better habits

Habits that keep drains clear without chemicals

Good habits do more than any home mixture. These are the small changes that make the biggest difference.

  • Scrape plates into the trash before you rinse them.
  • Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing.
  • Run hot water for thirty seconds after using the kitchen sink.
  • Use a mesh strainer in the sink and a hair catcher in every shower.
  • Never pour fat, coffee grounds or rice down the drain.
Why it comes back

What a returning clog tells you

A drain that clears and then clogs again is trying to tell you something. The cause may be a rough pipe wall that catches debris, a low spot in the line or roots at a joint. Treating the symptom with one mixture after another wastes time and lets the real problem grow.

When a clog returns more than once, ask a plumber to look with a camera. The footage shows what is happening inside the pipe, and the fix is often a single visit instead of a series of home treatments.

Kitchen versus bathroom

Different drains need different care

Kitchen drains collect grease, starch and food, and the cure is hot water, soap and a strainer. Bathroom sinks collect hair, toothpaste and soap scum, and a small hand auger or a hair tool pulls out most of it. Showers and tubs clog with hair above all else, so a catcher is the best tool.

Laundry drains clog with lint and soap, and a lint trap on the discharge hose helps. Floor drains in basements dry out and let sewer smell rise, and a cup of water poured in each month refills the trap.

Matching the method to the drain saves effort, and it shows when a plain clog is really a deeper problem.

Bottom line

The short version for a busy household

Use baking soda and vinegar for a monthly freshen up, not as a rescue. When a drain is slow in one fixture, try a plunger or a hand tool first. When two or more fixtures slow together, or water backs up, stop and call, since the cause is deeper than any home mixture can reach.
