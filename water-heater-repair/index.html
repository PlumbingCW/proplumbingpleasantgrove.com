The first things we check on a dead heater

Most no hot water calls come down to a short list. On an electric tank we test the breaker, the high limit reset and both heating elements. On a gas tank we look at the pilot or igniter, the thermocouple, the gas control valve and the vent.

Roughly half of the failures we see are cheap parts. The other half are tanks with a sediment-choked floor or a rusted shell that no part will fix, and we tell you which situation you are in before any parts are ordered.

Symptoms

What the heater is telling you

Each symptom points to a different cause, so describe it as exactly as you can on the phone.

  • No hot water at all: tripped breaker, failed thermostat, dead upper element or an out pilot.
  • Warm but not hot: a failed lower element, a broken dip tube or a thermostat set too low.
  • Water runs hot briefly then cold: failed element, or sediment burying the burner or element.
  • Rumbling or popping: scale on the tank bottom, which steals efficiency and can crack the liner.
  • Water dripping from the relief valve pipe: pressure or temperature building up, often from a failing expansion control.
  • Rotten egg smell from hot water only: a reaction at the anode rod that can be treated.
When to call now

A leaking water heater is an emergency

Water from the tank body, the base or a corroded fitting needs attention the same day. A slow drip from a pipe fitting can often be repaired. Water from the shell cannot.

  1. Shut the cold supply valve above the heater and turn the power or gas control off.
  2. Put a bucket or wet vacuum at the drip and watch whether it slows once the supply is closed.
  3. Do not touch the unit if water is near an electrical box or the floor around it is soaked.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from.

Preventive

Flushing, anode rods and Utah County water

Pleasant Grove's own 2023 report lists dissolved solids up to 396 parts per million, which means mineral scale is part of life here. Draining a few gallons from the bottom valve every year carries sediment out before it hardens.

An anode rod is the sacrificial part that protects the steel tank. If it is down to bare wire, replacing it can add years. We check it during any repair visit because it costs far less than a new tank.

Safety valve

The relief valve is a safety device, not a leak to ignore

A relief valve that drips or a discharge pipe that runs wet is telling you the tank is too hot or the pressure is too high. The IPC, which Utah applies statewide with amendments, requires this valve for a reason. Never cap the pipe to stop the drip, and call a plumber to find the cause.
