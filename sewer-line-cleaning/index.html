Emergency

Main line backup: the first ten minutes

A main line that fully blocks will push sewage into the lowest drain in the house. In many Pleasant Grove homes that is a basement floor drain or a shower, and sewage reaches finished flooring quickly.

  1. Stop all water use and flushing at once.
  2. Locate your main cleanout, usually a capped pipe outside near the foundation, and leave the cap on until the plumber arrives.
  3. Keep pets and children away from the affected room.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and ask the dispatcher about the cleanout location.
The work

How we clean a main line

A plumber opens the cleanout and feeds a large cable machine toward the city connection, cutting through roots, grease or debris. A jetter may follow to wash the walls clean. The plumber then runs the line again with water to confirm flow.

If there is no cleanout, the plumber may pull a toilet or access a roof vent. We recommend installing a cleanout during any repair, since it makes every later cleaning faster and cleaner.

Causes

What blocks a main line

Main line clogs follow a short list of causes, and the camera helps tell which applies.

  • Tree and shrub roots entering joints and cracks.
  • Grease, soap and wipes that build up and catch other debris.
  • A bellied or offset section that holds water and solids.
  • Scale and age in older cast iron and clay lines.
  • Items flushed that should not be, such as wipes sold as flushable.

Local factors

Backups in Pleasant Grove neighborhoods

Pleasant Grove homes connect to city mains that drain to the Timpanogos Special Service District treatment system. If neighbors on your street also have backups, the city main may be blocked and Pleasant Grove Public Works should be called as well as a plumber.

Homes near mature trees, such as those around Kiwanis Park and the older blocks near the historic downtown, see more root-related clogs. Seasonal storms can add stress, so a yearly cleaning is a smart habit for a root-prone line.

Code

The cleanout is part of the code for a reason

The International Plumbing Code, applied statewide in Utah with amendments, includes cleanout requirements so a blocked line can be opened without pulling a toilet. If your home has no cleanout, installing one is one of the best upgrades you can make to a main line.
